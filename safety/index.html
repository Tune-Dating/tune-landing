Tune Safety Tips

Last updated: November 20, 2026

These tips help you stay safe when you talk to a person on Tune and when you meet in person. They also say how to report a member, where to get help, and which safety measures we take. If you live in Colorado, also read our Colorado Safety Policy. In these tips, "Tune", "we", "us" and "our" mean Tunelab Technologies, Inc.

1. No criminal background checks

TUNE DOES NOT RUN CRIMINAL BACKGROUND CHECKS ON ITS MEMBERS, AND IT DOES NOT CHECK THEIR IDENTITY.

Use these tips with each person that you meet through Tune.

2. Online dating safety awareness

There is no substitute for acting with caution when communicating with any stranger who wants to meet you. Getting to know a person online has risks, so take precautions. Anyone who is able to commit identity theft can also falsify a dating profile, and a profile can hold false information.

  • Never include your last name, email address, home address, phone number, place of work, Social Security number or any other identifying information in your profile or in your first messages, and do not share it later until you feel comfortable with the other member.
  • Stop communicating with anyone who pressures you for personal or financial information or attempts in any way to trick or coerce you into revealing it.
  • Take your time to get to know a person before you agree to meet. When you feel comfortable with the person, a phone call or a video call before the meeting helps you to see that the person is who they say they are.
  • Be careful with a person who wants to move your conversation off Tune at once.
  • Unmatch and report a member who acts in a suspicious, offensive, threatening or fraudulent way, who harasses you, or who asks you for money or tries to sell you something (section 5).

3. Meeting in person: protecting yourself from sexual assault and dating violence

Sexual activity without consent is a crime.

If you choose to have a face-to-face meeting with another member, always tell someone in your family or a friend where you are going and when you will return. Never agree to be picked up at your home. Always provide your own transportation to and from your date and meet in a public place with many people around.

  • Keep your phone charged and with you, and think about sharing your location with a person you trust while you are out.
  • Know how alcohol and drugs affect you, and do not let anyone pressure you into more than you want.
  • Keep your drink and your things with you, and accept a drink only from the bartender or the server.
  • If you feel uncomfortable, leave, and ask the staff of the place for help if you need it.

4. Romance scams: protecting yourself from financial crimes

In a romance scam, a person builds a relationship with you online to get your money. Watch for these signs:

  • the person always has a reason why they cannot meet you in person;
  • the person asks you for money, for example for an emergency, a trip or a medical bill;
  • the person tells you how to pay, for example by a wire transfer, with gift cards, through a money transfer app or in cryptocurrency;
  • the person offers to help you invest, or asks you to open an account on a site or in an app that they choose.

Never send money or gift cards to a person whom you met online, even in an emergency, and above all never by a wire transfer, and never share information that gives access to your bank or other financial accounts. If you think that a member is a scammer, stop writing to them and report them (section 5). The Federal Trade Commission gives more advice at https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams, and you can report a scam to it at https://reportfraud.ftc.gov.

5. How to report

If you are in danger, call 911. You can report a member or content in the app. Report a member who breaks our Terms of Service, on Tune or off it. Tell us what happened as clearly as you can. You can also write to us through Help in the app. If a member commits a sexual crime, violence in a relationship or a financial crime against you, report it to us and to the police. A report can stop a person from harming you or others, and it lets us act against the member. A report can lead us to remove content, show an account to fewer people, suspend an account or ban it. If an intimate image of you was shared on Tune without your consent, ask us to remove it at https://tune.dating/take-it-down or by email to take-it-down@tune.dating. You do not need a Tune account for this request.

6. Where to get help

If you are in danger, call 911. If you experienced sexual assault, violence in a relationship or a financial crime, it is not your fault. You can get support from these hotlines and from services near you. These national services are free and open at any hour:

  • The National Domestic Violence Hotline, for people who experience domestic violence or abuse in a relationship: call 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788, or visit https://www.thehotline.org
  • RAINN's National Sexual Assault Hotline, for people who experience sexual assault or sexual harassment: call 1-800-656-4673, or chat at https://hotline.rainn.org/online

If you experienced a financial crime, section 4 says where to report it. If you are a member in Colorado, section 9 of our Colorado Safety Policy names services in Colorado.

7. Our safety measures

These measures lower risk. They do not check a member's identity or background, and they do not promise how a member acts (section 9 of our Terms of Service).

  • We do not open an account for a person whose date of birth shows that they are under 18, and we do not check that the date of birth is true.
  • Each member confirms a mobile phone number that can receive text messages.
  • Before a member sends their first message, we ask them to complete a face check: it checks that the person who writes is the person in the profile photos, and it is not an identity check, an age check or a background check.
  • We can refuse a person entry to a Tune event, or ask a person to leave it.
  • As soon as we can, and no later than 48 hours after we receive a valid request, we remove an intimate image that was shared on Tune without consent.

8. Child safety

Tune is only for adults aged 18 or older. We do not allow child sexual abuse material, or any content or conduct that sexualizes, grooms or exploits a child. If you see it on Tune, report it in the app at once (section 5), and if a child is in danger, call 911. When we find it, we remove it, ban the account, keep the records that the law requires and report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children, as federal law requires.
