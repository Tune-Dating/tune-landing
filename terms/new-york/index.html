New York Dating Service Consumer Bill of Rights

Last updated and in force from: November 5, 2026

This Dating Service Consumer Bill of Rights adds to our Terms of Service. It applies to you if you live in New York. It is the bill of rights that New York General Business Law section 394-c asks for. In this bill of rights, "Tune", "we", "us" and "our" mean Tunelab Technologies, Inc., and "you" means a person who lives in New York and buys a plan.

Your rights under New York law

  1. No contract for a social referral service can require you, the purchaser, to pay more than one thousand dollars. No such contract can run for more than two years. These two limits do not apply to a contract for an online dating service when its first term and each later term are one year or less, and a payment of more than one thousand dollars is reasonable for what the service offers.
  2. No contract for a social referral service can require you to buy a good or a service that is directly or indirectly related to the social referral service. Such extra services are known as ancillary services. The seller can offer you an ancillary service, but the law does not let the seller require you to buy it as a condition of your contract.
  3. If your contract costs more than twenty-five dollars, the seller must give you a minimum number of referrals each month, unless the service lets you search or shows you possible matches. If you do not get this minimum for two months in a row, you can cancel the contract. For an online dating service with a search function, or with matching by an algorithm or by location, you can cancel the contract if you get no referral for two months in a row. When you cancel, you get back all the money that you paid, less a cancellation fee. This fee cannot be more than either fifteen percent of the cash price or a pro rata amount for the number of referrals that you got.
  4. Unless your contract is for an online dating service that is generally available to users on a regional, national or global basis, your contract must state the distance that you are willing to travel to meet a referral. The seller must then give you no referral who lives farther from you than that distance.
  5. The provider must have an established policy for the case in which you move outside the area that it serves. This policy must be explained in your contract. Tune's policy is in section 20 of the Terms, under "If you live in Illinois, New York, North Carolina or Ohio, and you move".
  6. If a provision of your contract is violated, you have the right to bring a court action against the provider that violated the contract. Section 14 of the Terms says how a dispute between you and Tune is resolved.

How Tune sets its cancellation fee

Tune does not promise a number of people that it shows you. If Tune shows you no profile for two months in a row or more, you can cancel your plan. Our cancellation fee is then the smaller of two amounts: the part of the price for the time before you cancel, and fifteen percent of the price. You get back all that you paid, less that fee. Section 20 of the Terms, under "If you live in New York", gives this rule and says how to ask for the refund.

Putting your plan on hold

Section 20 of the Terms, under "If you live in New York", says how you can put your plan on hold for up to one year.
